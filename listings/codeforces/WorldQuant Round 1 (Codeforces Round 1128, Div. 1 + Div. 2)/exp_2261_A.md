# 2261A — Hey, It's Me, Souvlakity

[Problem](https://codeforces.com/contest/2261/problem/A) · [C++17 code](comp_2261_A.cpp)

## Problem

The positions of `1` in a binary string are the integers Souvlakity owns. On his turn he gives one integer away; on Kalamakity's turn he receives one. After `10^100` alternating turns, Souvlakity maximizes his final maximum minus minimum, while Kalamakity minimizes it.

The enormous turn count is even. Group the game into complete pairs of turns.

## Solution

Let `count` be the number of ones, and `first` and `last` their extreme positions.

| Initial count | Answer |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 1 |
| At least 3 | `last - first` |

A single scan finds all three quantities.

## Why it works

Every complete pair preserves Souvlakity's number of integers.

With one integer, the final range is zero. With two, after Souvlakity's move only one integer remains. Kalamakity owns every other integer and can return a neighbor of that integer, obtaining range one. Repeating this strategy keeps the range one; two distinct integers cannot have a smaller range.

With at least three, Souvlakity can always donate an integer other than his minimum and maximum. His range cannot decrease. Conversely, Kalamakity can immediately return every donated integer, keeping the initial set unchanged after every pair. These strategies give matching lower and upper bounds equal to the initial range.

For `101010`, the extreme ones are at positions 1 and 5, so the answer is 4. For `1100`, the answer is 1 because there are only two ones.

## Complexity

- Time: `O(n)` per test case.
- Extra space: `O(1)` beyond the input string.

## Execution tips and common mistakes

- Do not simulate the turns or use a big-integer representation of `10^100`.
- Compute the count and both extremes in the same scan.
- Use the same indexing convention for both extremes. Their difference works with either zero-based or one-based indices.
- The two-one case needs its own answer even if the initial ones are very far apart.
- Fast iostream settings and `'\n'` avoid unnecessary per-line flushing.

## Verification

The case analysis proves the result for any positive number of complete pairs. The package checks the formula against minimax on small arrays and includes the six official examples.
