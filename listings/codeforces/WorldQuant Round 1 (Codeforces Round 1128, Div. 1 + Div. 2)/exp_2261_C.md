# 2261C — I Know About a Million Things

[Problem](https://codeforces.com/contest/2261/problem/C) · [C++17 code](comp_2261_C.cpp)

## Problem

Players alternately remove a nonempty subset. A move is legal when it removes exactly one element, or when the removed sum is even. Whoever receives an empty array loses. Moggity moves first.

## Solution

Count the odd elements. Print `Larpity` only for:

1. Exactly two elements, one odd and one even.
2. Exactly three elements, all odd.

Print `Moggity` in every other case.

## Why it works

Only parity matters: a subset has even sum exactly when it contains an even number of odd elements.

The two listed states are losing for the player to move. From one odd and one even, only a singleton can be removed, leaving a singleton for the opponent. From three odds, a legal move removes one or two elements; the opponent can remove everything remaining.

Every other nonempty state can win immediately or move to one of those losing states:

- An even number of odd elements gives an even total sum: remove the entire array.
- A single odd element with no evens is a singleton: remove it.
- Exactly one odd with at least two evens: remove all but one even, leaving one odd and one even.
- An odd number of odds at least three, with some additional element beyond three odds: remove everything except three odds. The removed odd count is even, so the move is legal.

For example, `[2, 3]` is losing for Moggity. `[1, 3, 5, 7, 9]` is winning: remove two odds and leave three odds.

## Complexity

- Time: `O(n)` per test case, required to read the input.
- Extra space: `O(1)`.

## Execution tips and common mistakes

- Read and count parity directly; do not store, sort, or sum the values.
- A single odd is removable because singleton moves are explicitly allowed.
- Odd total sum alone does not imply a losing position.
- Even if the answer is apparent early, read all remaining input values before the next test case.
- Match the output names exactly: `Moggity` and `Larpity`.

## Verification

The classification matched exhaustive minimax over every odd/even count combination with 1 through 40 elements: 860 states.
