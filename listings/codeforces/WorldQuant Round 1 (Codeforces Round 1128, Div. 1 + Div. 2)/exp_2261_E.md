# 2261E — What Does the Cow Say?

[Problem](https://codeforces.com/contest/2261/problem/E) · [C++17 code](comp_2261_E.cpp)

## Problem

Given a regular bracket sequence, one operation removes an adjacent `()` and adds `(` at the beginning and `)` at the end. After at most `k` operations, maximize the longest consecutive run of opening brackets.

## Compress the sequence

There are `m = n/2` closing brackets. Let `c[i]` count the consecutive openings immediately before the `i`-th closing bracket; it may be zero. For example, `(()(()))` becomes `[2,2,0,0]`.

An operation deletes a closing bracket and the opening immediately before it. That opening is moved to the front. This makes a run at the front behave differently from an interior run.

## Prefix case

Delete the first `i-1` original closing brackets, in order. The removed openings return to the beginning, so no opening is lost from the growing prefix. It contains `sum(c[1..i])` openings.

Each remaining operation can move another opening from outside the prefix to its front, until all `m` openings are there. For every `i-1 <= k`, evaluate:

```text
min(m, sum(c[1..i]) + k - (i-1))
```

The RBS prefix condition ensures the required left-to-right deletions are legal.

## Interior case

Merging runs `l..r` removes `r-l` intervening closing brackets and the same number of openings from the interior run. Its candidate score is:

```text
sum(c[l..r]) - (r-l), with r-l <= k
```

Define `P[i] = sum(c[j]-1, j=1..i)`, with `P[0]=0`. The score becomes `1 + P[r] - P[l-1]`. For a fixed `r`, maximize it by finding the minimum `P[p]` in:

```text
max(0, r-k-1) <= p <= r-1
```

A monotonic deque maintains this minimum. Remove dominated indices at the back, append `r-1`, and expire old indices at the front.

## Why the cases are sufficient

Newly prepended openings can help only the prefix run. An interior run consists of original openings whose intervening closing brackets were deleted, so the interior-window expression covers it.

The selected minimum-prefix window also admits legal deletions: every intermediate prefix sum is at least its starting minimum. Thus at least one opening remains immediately available before each closing bracket that must be removed. Algebraic windows with an unusable starting prefix cannot improve on the window chosen by the deque.

Taking the maximum over prefix candidates and deque candidates gives the optimum.

## Complexity

- Time: `O(n)`; every deque index enters and leaves at most once.
- Space: `O(n)`.

## Execution tips and common mistakes

- Record zero-length runs for consecutive `)`; skipping them changes operation costs.
- A window of `k+1` runs costs `k` operations: the bound has an extra one.
- The weights `c[i]-1` can be negative, so a fixed-length window is insufficient.
- Handle prefix gains separately; charging one lost opening for prefix operations underestimates the answer.
- Reserve space for `c` and cap prefix scores at `m`.
- `k=0` naturally returns the original longest run; no special branch is required.
